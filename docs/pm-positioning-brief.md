# PM Positioning Brief

> As of 2026-10-30 the site is positioned for software engineering roles. The product-manager framing below is historical and is not current guidance; AGENTS.md records the current positioning.

## Core positioning

Cody Johnson is a full stack software engineer working in C#/.NET, TypeScript, React, and SQL, with product ownership and business operations experience behind the engineering. That is the resume's positioning, chosen by the owner in October 2026 over the earlier technical product owner framing; AGENTS.md records the decision and the files that carry it. The portfolio should show that he can turn ambiguous asks into scoped, shippable work and then show the result holding up: requirements and acceptance criteria, the APIs and integrations behind them, validation paths, and the disconnect, restart, and duplicate-event handling underneath real-time and operational systems.

Where the same work can be framed as a business outcome or as investigation, validation, and system behavior, the second framing wins.

## Audience

- Recruiters screening for full stack, C#/.NET, and other software engineering roles.
- Hiring managers looking for cross-functional execution, backlog discipline, and stakeholder communication.
- Interviewers testing whether Cody can translate messy needs into deliverable work with engineering teams.

## Narrative spine

1. Lead with the operating pattern: clarify, scope, sequence, ship, validate.
2. Use active products as proof of current product judgment.
3. Use Lambda Curry as the strongest cross-functional product-owner case.
4. Use HSNBA, Attend, RideSense, and Wattsmith as proof of operational reliability, validation, metrics, and trust-building.
5. Frame engineering fluency as a way to reduce ambiguity and communicate better with technical teams.

## Schwab-aligned product-owner signals

- Cross-functional delivery: Lambda Curry, Attend, active product work.
- User stories and acceptance criteria: Lambda Curry and resume content.
- Backlog sequencing: Lambda Curry, Wattsmith roadmap, Trackday Tuner prioritization.
- Platform health and issue awareness: Attend delivery failure alerts, RideSense sync hardening, Trackday Tuner AI observability, Wattsmith export readiness.

- Metrics and operational reporting: Lambda Curry outcomes, Attend Command Center, HSNBA data-quality outcomes.

## Messaging rules

- Lead with products in active build and the operator mindset behind them.
- Lead with engineering: the hero eyebrow and the preloader open with the full stack software engineer and C#/.NET positioning, and product ownership appears as the experience behind it rather than as the title.
- Prefer "reproduced the failure," "defined what happens when it breaks," and "validated it against real data" over tool-by-tool descriptions.
- Use metrics where available, but do not manufacture them.
- Keep the site selective and product-first.

## Proof themes

### Product thinking grounded in delivery

The user does not only produce ideas. He can scope, ship, and support execution with engineering teams.

### Customer and revenue awareness

The background includes product delivery, retail, and operational work where customer behavior and commercial outcomes are visible and actionable.

### Technical fluency without losing the user story

The portfolio should make it clear that technical knowledge helps with prioritization and tradeoffs. It should not turn into an engineer demo reel.

### Calm under ambiguity

Client delivery and operational environments show the ability to build clarity where process is thin or moving quickly.

## Tone

- Credible
- Direct
- Polished
- Outcome-oriented

## What to cut

- Generic "problem solver" positioning
- Skills, strengths, and toolkit lists: the resume tells, the site shows. Capability areas belong inside project copy as evidence that the work happened, which is also why `/resume` stays unrouted.
- Empty portfolio filler
- Technology lists with no connection to outcomes
- Case studies that are really just build logs
